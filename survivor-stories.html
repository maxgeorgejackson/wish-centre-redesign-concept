Survivor Stories — Making a Difference

These stories are shared with permission to help others understand what support can look like, and to show funders and partners the real impact of this work. Some details are changed to protect identities.

Emma's story

Emma experienced physical abuse and sexual exploitation as a child, and later faced partner abuse during pregnancy. Trusting services wasn't easy at first, given her past trauma, but she gradually engaged with support from an IDVA and an outreach worker. After moving into a mother-and-baby placement and securing her own tenancy, she achieved safety and independence. Emma now volunteers with The Wish Centre, using her lived experience to support others going through the same thing.

Aisha's story

A family was relocated into refuge after the mother reported child sexual abuse by the paternal grandfather. The mother had already experienced extensive trauma in her home country, including her brother's kidnapping and her own mother's murder. Within her marriage she endured emotional, verbal and physical abuse, and coercive control, spanning a decade. Since entering refuge, she has engaged with multiple support services, obtained a Non-Molestation Order, and begun education and community integration — though concerns about future contact arrangements through the family courts remain ongoing.

Memories from Childhood

A poem contributed by a 52-year-old man, reflecting on witnessing domestic abuse inflicted on his mother during his own childhood — a reminder of how this trauma can echo for decades, and why early support for children matters.

These accounts involve IDVA support, refuge accommodation, trauma-informed care, safety planning and multi-agency working — the same services described on our Services page.
